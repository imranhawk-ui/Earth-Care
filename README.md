# Earth-Care
EARTH CARE
EARTH CARE SATELLITE is a space mission that was launched by Japan along with the European Space Agency to figure out how clouds and aerosols affect the temperature on Earth. It is in LEO(Low Earth Orbit) and sunsynchronous satellite, which means it travels the equator at the exact same time every solar day. This is also a problem since it cant find the cloud movement over a single area for 24 hours.  Aerosol is a product, which I experienced working with using Aerosol watch data from TEMPO satellite by NASA, actually cools the temperature. Aerosol act as condensation seeds from water droplets, the seeds themselves being Dust particles, and other pollutants. Water droplets get around these particles and turn into liquid cloud droplets.
The Website
As I went through the discussion on the website earth.esa.int I found out various mission products like L1 and L2, JAXA L2 which are made by collaboration of Japan and ESA. The Orbit Data gives the navigational positioning of the satellite.  The XMET product helps to match the sensor data by reducing the data volume by almost 20.  The good thing is that Earth Care satellite data is available for everyone’s use.
The Satellite uses narrow straw instead of the global swath which I think will prevent it from having better data over wider region. But what is impressive is the fact it uses vertical cross section of the clouds to create a 3D profile. It gives high resolution cloud layers, i.e. the low clouds, mid clouds and high clouds and even Cumulonimbus.

4 Major Parts
•	The Atmospheric Light Detection and Ranging developed by ESA which sends UV rays to find very small aerosol detections. 
•	The Cloud profiling Radar uses doppler effect to find velocity of clouds. Doppler effect has been studied since high school and even in this case is helpful since when the radar sends a radiowave pulse and it hits the rain drop and returns back. The frequency change, depending on which direction the rain drop moves, gives the doppler shift. One thing worth mentioning is the fact that sice the rain drops are not spherical(which would have been helpful since we could use Mie scattering) it causes delay in signal. This attenuation is actually solved by the T matrix. It is basically the Scattered wave coefficient divided by the Incident Wave coefficient. It considers the non spherical nature of the rain drop. It even allows bands to identify the ice crystal wether it is snowflake or something else.
•	The Multi Spectral Imager, as already said, describes the high resolution context map of cloud layers. Although it is powerful, teams in the project have found difficulty calibrating it. 
•	The Broadband Radiometer corrects any error in the other three instruments by checking the data and measures the actual reflected light and heat radiating away from Earth.
Recent achievement of this satellite was the detection of Category 5 Typhoon Sinlaku in Western Pacific.
Disadvantages
Among all the disadvantages I already mentioned, one is also the that of durability and maintenance. Like any LEO satellite its lifespan is short, and thus more durable versions need to be made along with it being a global swath satellite which will solve the problem due to its narrow straw sensors which measures only 30 meters. Another issue being latency that is processing delay which is 24-48 hours. To check if the data is correct or not scientist have been working extensively thorugh Ground-Based Calibration and Validation observations which have high quality LiDAR and RADAR. Such ground stations are NASA AERONET, Europe’s EARLINET, and in Japan the NICT headquarters.
